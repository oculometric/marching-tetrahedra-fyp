# Introduction
This paper will compare several variations in approach to extraction of isosurfaces from 3-dimensional scalar functions. It will evaluate the demonstrated algorithms for their fitness specifically as applied to real-time procedural mesh generation for non-height-map terrain in videogames. This places a number of restrictions and goals on the algorithm to be used:
1. The algorithm must be able to generate a mesh directly from a provided scalar function.
2. The algorithm should generate manifold, closed geometry.
3. The algorithm should be highly optimised for speed, with minimal startup time.
4. The algorithm should produce geometry with minimal triangle count.
5. The algorithm should produce geometry which avoids shading artifacts when using smooth shading such as Phong. !CITATION!
For real-time rendering applications such as videogames, triangle counts should be reduced where possible to minimise GPU utilisation. Since terrain meshes often take on organic forms !CITATION!, they can benefit greatly from regularisation techniques which are able to distribute triangles and eliminate small faces. Extremely small or narrow faces which often result from isosurface extraction algorithms are wasteful both due to overall triangle count, and as a result of overdraw. Nearly all modern graphics acceleration hardware shades pixels in 2x2 chunks, often called 'quads' !CITATION!. Overdraw occurs when wasted work is performed due to a triangle only covering a portion of a quad, resulting in pixels being shaded many more times than is necessary and contributing to worse performance. The presence of tiny or elongated triangles in an organic mesh also results in shading problems !DEMONSTRATE WITH A PAIR OF IMAGES! which significantly degrade the appearance of geometry shaded using interpolated normals (such as Phong).

In addition to this, the performance of the isosurface extraction algorithm is of great importance. Many videogames involve player-modifiable worlds, which may include terrain which is initially procedurally generated, such as !ASTRONEER!. In order to facilitate this, two considerations are made: first, that the algorithm should be capable of evaluating a usefully-sized volume of space within a typical game frame-time (1/60 of a second); second, that the algorithm should be capable of evaluating a larger volume as a series of smaller blocks if necessary. Modifiable terrain is unlikely to be altered in many places at once, and thus not all of the space must be re-tesselated for every modification.

The final key consideration for the algorithm is that it reliably produces manifold, closed geometry. A manifold (in this case, a 2-manifold) implies a surface which does not intersect itself, where its surface is appears locally Euclidian at all points !CITATION!. For videogames, these restrictions produce a mesh which does not appear to cross itself (which would usually be considered non-physical and thus inappropriate for virtual terrain), which does not instantaneously invert its orientation (normal vectors vary smoothly across the surface), which has a defined inside and outside (as conveyed by the direction of the surface normals), and which has no loose geometry or holes, except where the evaluation space terminates. All of these conditions are necessary to produce geometry which may be perceived by players as physically solid, as single-sided holes, self-intersections, and surfaces viewable from unexpected angles (due to inverted normal vectors) all break the illusion of a solid object.

This paper will examine the literature in relation to relevant isosurface extraction methods, and will implement the landmark 'regularised marching tetrahedra' algorithm initially presented by Treece, Prager, and Gee (1999) !CITATION!. An alternative arrangement of the tetrahedral lattice, and an alternative mesh simplification algorithm will be presented and the impact of these variations will be examined with regard to the evaluation time, triangle count and mean aspect ratio, and perceived visual quality of resulting meshes.
